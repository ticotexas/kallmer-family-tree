## The Cable Plant

After four or five years back in Omaha, I was assigned as the SQC engineer for the cable plant, an area where I had no experience, and I covered that area for the balance of my career.

We made vinyl-covered cable for use in central offices, exchange cable for underground use, and all the wire for those, plus many other wire types.

## Bridge and the Stock Market

In my late twenties I learned to play bridge and to navigate the stock market. Both efforts have been very rewarding.

## AT&T Divestiture

At work, the government-ordered AT&T divestiture in 1984 caused major new work for Western Electric. Among other things, we were required to develop a rigorous program to assure calibration for all measuring equipment and establish an interval fitting its design and usage.

Our old system was a combination of user requests and equipment failure, and it didn't include all devices. I was assigned to design and implement the new system. With a lot of help from the computer folks, we got it done.

## The Quality Manual

I was also assigned to put together a comprehensive quality manual for the Omaha Works describing all elements of our quality control program. It was to be notebook-sized, indexed, and all eventual owners furnished a serially numbered copy, allowing any revisions to be sent to the registered owners. I had help, but wrote most of it myself.

## Audits and Downsizing

My last years at Western Electric were spent designing audits for quality systems. That was kinda fun, as I got to make up questions to be asked of various employees about the work they do.

As time went on, the manufacture of telephone equipment, at least the stuff we made in Omaha, was replaced by things like fiber optics in place of copper cable, and the 50-pound electromechanical central office switches we made were replaced by chips the size of a fingernail. We continually downsized.

## Retirement

Finally, in late 1989 the company made a great offer of enhanced pensions for those of us who were eligible, and I retired, along with a lot of others considered management. I left on December 30, 1989, at age 51.

I've never regretted leaving at that time.
