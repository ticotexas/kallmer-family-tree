## A New Home

I left the Millard house on January 1, 1993, and moved to 7625 Briggs Street in Omaha, the two-bedroom apartment I had rented for the girls a year earlier. It was just a few blocks southwest of 72nd and Pacific.

Sheri had graduated from Creighton School of Nursing at the end of 1992 and was moving to Fort Collins, Colorado, so I just took her room. Sunny and I shared the apartment for about a year and a half.

In September 1993 I bought a six-lot parcel, one half of a city block, near downtown Wahoo. During the following winter I decided how to site a home on the lots, sketched out a floor plan, and found a contractor to build it.

Construction began on March 1, 1994, breaking through three feet of frost that had accumulated during the winter. I moved in that July, and my address was established as 656 North Orange Street, where I am now.

## Sunny Moves to Texas

At the end of 1994 Sunny graduated from the University of Nebraska Medical Center with a master's-level degree as a Physician Assistant. I helped her with a winter move to Coleman, Texas, where she had accepted a position at a clinic.

On my way back to Nebraska I was surprised at how early all the Casey's and other convenience stores closed for the evening. I found out later it was because they weren't allowed to sell beer after those hours. I had barely enough gas to cross into Nebraska.

## Dating Again

I had joined Omaha Christian Singles. I dated quite a few gals and took many dance lessons, where I also met a lot of single women.

No details here, but I can tell you that the world of middle-aged divorcees and widows is **VERY** interesting.

I met several women who had been married and divorced three times and took absolutely no responsibility for the results. They had simply married three big jerks.

I was briefly engaged—no more than a month—to such a woman named Sandy in early 1997.

## Lorraine

In the fall of that year I met and started dating Lorraine Patton, who had been one year behind me at Wahoo High School. Her first husband, Bill Patton, had died in 1993. Bill had also grown up in Wahoo, lived about four blocks from me, and was someone with whom I had enjoyed many poker games.

Lorraine and I were engaged for about two years and married on January 1, 2000, giving me four stepchildren and five step-grandchildren. There are now ten grandchildren in that branch of the family.

In mid-2000 we tore down the old one-car garage and carport on the property and built the three-and-a-half-car garage that we now call the Carriage House.

## Life Together

In the years since then we have driven to the West Coast at least four times, covering Washington, Oregon, and California. We've also traveled through the South, made many trips to Texas and Arizona, once to Florida and Maine, and a few times to Tennessee, Kansas, Iowa, Wyoming, the Dakotas, Minnesota, and Wisconsin.

We've seen many museums, arboretums, automobile collections, and a host of Nebraska's delightful places.

We've also welcomed into the family five more grandchildren, five (soon to be six) great-grandchildren, and a number of sons-in-law.

We have enjoyed many years together improving our bridge partnership, and we have competed in both duplicate and rubber bridge.

At the present time we are talking about downsizing, but talking about it is as far as it goes.

As of August 2026, we remain in pretty good health and will probably remain here at home until that changes.

_Bob Urban_
