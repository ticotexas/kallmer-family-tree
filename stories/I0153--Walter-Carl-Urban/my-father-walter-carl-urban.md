Walter Carl Urban, son of John and Anna Urban, was born August 17, 1901, at Chalco, Nebraska.

He grew up on his parents' farms in the Uehling, Hooper, and Oakland, Nebraska, area and received most of his education in the Uehling and Hooper public schools. At age 16, he was confirmed in St. Paul Lutheran Church in Uehling. He later joined the Methodist Church in Wahoo in 1946.

In 1921, his parents moved to a farm southeast of Ashland, where Walter farmed with his brothers until he went to work for Roberts Dairy Company in Lincoln.

On July 21, 1928, he married Zella Louise Ullstrom of Lincoln, who also worked at Roberts Dairy. They were both employed there until March 1933, when they moved to Los Angeles, California.

Their son, Walter Robert, was born there on May 11, 1938.

Walter worked for Arden Farms Dairy in Alhambra. During World War II, he occasionally walked to work because of gasoline rationing. His delivery route included Beverly Hills and the Hollywood Hills, where he got to know numerous movie stars.

After 12 years in Los Angeles, the family moved back to Nebraska, where Walter was given the job of managing the Wahoo Locker Plant. After cutting meat there for 15 years, he left in 1960 to take a job as a chauffeur at the General Dynamics installation at the Mead Missile Base.

He retired at about age 65 to draw his Social Security benefit. With his sunny disposition, people continued to seek him out for part-time work. He cut meat at the Langemeyer locker and store in Mead, worked at the Fairview Cafe, and did janitorial work at the Wahoo Court House. In later years, he also served on a jury there.

Walter and Zella enjoyed traveling to the Southwest, fishing trips with his friends George and Ed Vybiral, helping with painting and other projects at his son Bob's home in Millard, golfing, and playing cards at the Wahoo Eagles Club.

Walter died of heart disease on July 10, 1973, at age 71. His wife and son survived him.

Bob later added one correction to the story of his parents' early years. He did not think they had first met at Roberts Dairy. He remembered his mother talking about knowing Walter from a neighboring farm and believed they had known each other for years before they married.

Bob also remembered one small regret of his own: he had always wanted to serve on a jury. He was called once, but the attorneys did not want any engineers for that particular case.

*Source: Family history and personal recollections written by Walter Robert “Bob” Urban and supplied to Tim Kallmer.*
