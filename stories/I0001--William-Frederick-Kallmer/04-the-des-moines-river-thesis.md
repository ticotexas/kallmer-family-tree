During that summer, I got a job with Harrison and Ripple, a landscape
architectural office in Nevada, Iowa. This firm had some clients along
the East Coast, and they piloted their own airplane.

On one trip, I flew to the East with them. They dropped me off, and I
interviewed with several landscape architectural firms from Baltimore to
White Plains, New York, then to Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. The plan was
for me to meet up with them and fly back to Ames with them.

They called me and said that because of bad weather, they were not
flying their plane, but instead we all flew home on commercial flights.
After the weather got better, one of the owners flew back to recover his
plane. I always appreciated that decision, for it told me safety comes
first.

For my Master's degree, I was the first student in the Landscape
Architectural Department to do a thesis on a project. With help from my
advisor, Robert W. Dyas, I secured my thesis project from the Iowa State
Conservation Commission and was paid to do it.

The project for the State was to research, analyze, and document the
benefits of the Des Moines River valley from Fort Dodge to Madrid, Iowa.
My thesis report was titled *Recreational Analysis of the Des Moines
River*. It consisted of 50 copies with 54 pages of written information,
along with colored maps of the river valley.

I purchased a second car, a 1956 Mercury, and traveled the gravel roads
along that part of the river. On one occasion, Mr. Dyas and I canoed a
portion of the river and camped for the night along the riverbank.

Once, hiking up a rocky creek bed, I looked down near my feet and
immediately saw an Indian ax head. I have it to this day. Many Indian
burial mounds are found throughout the length of the river.

Mr. Dyas also arranged for the State of Iowa to take both of us and fly
the river in a single-engine airplane. I was excited to fly that
morning, so I got up early and cooked a bacon-and-egg breakfast. I had
the front seat next to the pilot, and Mr. Dyas was behind me.

For most of the trip, I was doing fine. However, when we came back to
land, the plane was moving sideways a lot and I was feeling sick. Yes,
it did happen, and I messed up the instrument panel and other things.

After graduating in November 1967, we purchased a new 1968 Camaro, and
U-Haul hooked a small trailer to the car's bumper. Not a good idea, but
what did we know? We took our few belongings, including our dog and a
bedroom set, and headed to Baltimore, Maryland.
