I was the youngest son of Floyd and Edith Kallmer of Storm Lake, Iowa,
born on July 31, 1942. My other two brothers, Bob and Jim, and I had
free rein in Storm Lake in those early years. My dad would just say,
"Make sure you're home at 5 o'clock for dinner."

I would explore downtown shops and the 3,000-acre lake; go swimming;
walk the Indian trails at Sunset Park; climb trees and cut a crotch
branch for making a slingshot; camp at the west end of the lake with
school friends; help raise hamsters, fish, a rabbit, a parakeet, dogs, a
cat, and two baby pigeons in my dad's garage; drop water balloons from
Dad's tree when people walked by; play hockey at the city park in
below-freezing temperatures until I couldn't feel my feet, then Dad put
my feet in a bucket of snow to thaw them out; wreck my dad's cars; and
hunt ground squirrels, timber squirrels, pheasants, and rabbits.

Yes, those were the good old days that I enjoyed.
