In January 1968, I took a job with the firm Knecht and Human in
Cockeysville, Maryland. My starting pay at that time was $4.00 per
hour. That was a fair wage because I quickly found out I didn't know
what I was doing.

Within a few days, we rented a unit in a six-unit row house at 1257 East
Belvedere Avenue. LaDon was lonely in our rented house with no
furniture, so she took a job with the Chesapeake and Potomac Telephone
Co.

That was the year, 1968, that Dr. Martin Luther King was assassinated.
That was a troubling time for our nation and for two Iowa kids.

In 1969, LaDon and I had our son, Timothy Layne, and in 1973, we had our
daughter, Cherlyn Janel. In 1970, we purchased half of a duplex unit at
7608 Daniel Avenue.

When the economy slowed in 1970, I was let go, but the company kept me
on until I found another job. I found one within a week in downtown
Baltimore from a church friend who told me about the firm he was working
for. This firm was Gaudreau Architects, Inc.

I had a great experience and learned a lot from a boss, Bob Krebs, who
was a professional civil engineer. My one big project was to develop the
layout, drawings, and specifications for the athletic fields for Towson
State College. This project involved a quarter-mile track, shot put,
steeplechase water jump, pole vault, discus, long and triple jump, high
jump, football field, softball field, tennis courts, soccer field,
lacrosse field, along with site grading, road paving, parking areas,
storm drainage, sanitary sewer, and potable water.

While working on a multi-firm project in Silver Springs, Maryland, I met
an out-of-state engineer who was employed at Henningson, Durham, and
Richardson (HDR) in Omaha, Nebraska. In our conversation, I explained my
work at Gaudreau. He told me that I should send my resume to HDR in
order to inquire about a position with them.

That proved successful, so LaDon, the children, the dog, and I moved to
Omaha in May of 1976.
