On June 6, 1964, LaDon and I got married in my junior year. We found an
upper-level apartment at 2823 Lincoln Way. LaDon's mother raised
purebred cocker spaniels, and we took one with us to Ames. His name was
Kipper.

LaDon worked downtown for Northwestern Bell Telephone, and she became
the business office supervisor. She helped pay my university bills. See,
I am smart.

At school, I had several jobs and stayed in Ames during the summer. In
the Horticulture Department, I worked for Griffith J. Buck, a well-known
international expert in the rose community. I worked in the greenhouse
breeding geraniums, collecting and potting rose and geranium seeds, and
taking care of rose plants on the horticulture farm.

The farm had a large open pasture, so I would take my .22-caliber Ruger
pistol, lie low, and shoot ground squirrels. Nearby was a housing
development, but I was shooting in the opposite direction.

One day while I was there shooting, I noticed a police car drive along
the road adjacent to the field and stop where my car was parked. Moments
later, he continued driving on. I quickly packed up my gun and drove
home.

Soon there was a knock on our door, and it was the police. The
homeowners reported to the police that I must be the one shooting holes
in a cattle water tank. I told them it was not me and they should just
check the ballistics. Ya sure, on a .22 bullet. Stupid me, not smart
this time.

They took my gun and gave me a ride to the downtown police station. In
the station, I remembered, and I told them, that I thought the gun was
still loaded. They checked, and it was. Stupid again.

When I was released from jail (that didn't happen) with no fine, the
police gave me a ride home.

Back at work at the Horticulture Department, one job was to strip and
refinish a large wooden office cooler that was used to keep cut flowers
fresh. After doing that job, I printed on a postcard, "THAT WAS ONE HELL
OF A JOB," along with my name, my major, and the date, and placed it
behind the cooler.

Many years later, the cooler was replaced and the postcard was found and
returned to me. LaDon wasn't happy that I wrote HELL.

After all that, I graduated in 1966 with a BLA degree, then stayed in
school to earn my Master's Degree in Landscape Architecture.
