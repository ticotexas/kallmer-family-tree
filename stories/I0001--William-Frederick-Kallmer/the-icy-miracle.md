## A Narrow Escape on the Road to Sioux City

_Recounted by William (Bill) Kallmer_

## February 15, 1964

Early on the morning of **February 15, 1964**, with a chill in the air and about a half inch of snow covering an icy road, Bill Kallmer, LaDon, and Aunt Kate set out for Sioux City in a **1954 Buick**. They were traveling to attend Bill's father's wedding.

Bill was driving, with LaDon in the front passenger seat and Aunt Kate seated in the back. They headed south on **Highway 110** along the west side of the lake before turning west onto **State Road C63 (610th Street)**.

Within a couple of miles, they approached a hill. As they neared the crest, they were startled to see a large box truck coming toward them. Even worse, another car was fully committed to passing the truck. Together, the two oncoming vehicles occupied the entire width of the narrow paved road.

As the distance between the three vehicles rapidly disappeared, none of the drivers panicked. On the snow-covered pavement, applying the brakes would almost certainly have caused a skid. There was only one possible course of action.

Bill held the Buick steady and aimed directly down the center of the road.

When the two eastbound drivers realized what Bill was doing, they immediately understood what they had to do as well. The truck driver eased partly onto the narrow shoulder to his left, while the passing car moved onto the shoulder to Bill's right.

All three vehicles passed one another at exactly the same moment.

After they cleared each other, Bill looked in his mirror and saw the passing car partly in the ditch. Moments later, the car reached a farm entrance, which allowed it to climb back onto the shoulder.

Bill stopped briefly to make sure everyone had made it safely.

They had.

No one else stopped, and each vehicle continued on its way.

Aunt Kate remained as cool as a cucumber throughout the ordeal. LaDon, understandably, was badly shaken.

How all three vehicles managed to avoid a collision on that icy February morning is difficult to explain.

Perhaps it was simply an icy miracle.
