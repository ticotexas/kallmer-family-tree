When I was five years old, I attended kindergarten at Storm Lake Public
School. The following year, I attended St. Mary's Catholic School.

At seven years old, a doctor told my parents that young Bill had
rheumatic fever. I was not allowed to attend school or walk much, and I
stayed most of the day in my parents' bed. In their bed, I did artwork
and stared at the ceiling until my eyes went out of focus many times.

After being bedridden for nine months, I recovered. However, I had to do
second grade again. That ended up being a good thing because I was now
the oldest in my class and much smarter than my classmates, and I was in
the same grade as my future wife.

Both my brothers and I played basketball and other sports. As a
freshman, I made the basketball varsity team, as well as the other three
years. For one year, when Bob was a senior, Jim a junior, and I was a
freshman, we were all on the varsity team and played some games at the
same time. I consider brother Bob the best of us all.

During his senior year, I met the love of my life, LaDon Raye Huffey, a
beautiful blonde from public school---a girl above my pay grade. We
dated for several years, from 1960 to 1964.

After high school graduation in 1961, I attended Loras College in
Dubuque, Iowa. My dad insisted that all three boys must attend Loras
College our first year. The next year, and the following years in
college, I attended Iowa State University in Ames and majored in
Landscape Architecture. My brother Jim was instrumental in steering me
to that occupation.

I didn't have a car at college, so to get home to my gal, I would
hitchhike or take the train. One time hitchhiking, a down-and-out man
picked me up. He told me he didn't have a job and wanted to know what my
dad did for a living. About that time, we were in Storm Lake on Lake
Avenue. I did not answer his question, but rather said, "You can let me
out here." He stopped and I got out of his car. I was relieved. I then
walked a block to get home.

Getting back to school was by taking the train. LaDon would see me off
at the depot with tears in her eyes.
