*November 11, 1939 – April 16, 2010*

Robert C. “Bob” Kallmer was born in Storm Lake, Iowa, to Floyd F. and Edith L. Kallmer. From Storm Lake, his life would eventually carry him across Europe, the Middle East, Africa, India, and Southeast Asia.

Bob graduated from St. Mary’s High School in Storm Lake in 1958. He attended Loras College in Dubuque for two years, then spent his third year of college in Germany at the University of Saarbrücken. The experience seems to have strengthened an already growing sense of adventure and curiosity about the wider world.

After returning to the United States, Bob graduated from the University of Iowa in 1962 with a B.A. and a major in German. He soon returned to Germany, teaching English in Augsburg, Bavaria, during the 1963–64 school year. When the school year ended, he spent six weeks traveling through Germany and Sweden by bicycle, covering some 1,800 miles.

That trip to Sweden had special meaning. There Bob met relatives on his mother Edith’s side of the family and learned enough Swedish to speak and read the language at a respectable level.

Bob returned to the University of Iowa for the 1964–65 school year intending to earn a master’s degree in German, but before the year was over he grew restless. He applied for the Peace Corps.

After the school year, he traveled to New York City and interviewed with IBM. He was hired and, within several months, had already received a promotion. Then the Peace Corps offer arrived. To the surprise of his boss and co-workers, Bob left IBM and accepted the assignment.

His first Peace Corps posting took him to a small city in Iran, where he taught English to high school students for two years. As he would repeatedly do throughout his life, Bob immersed himself in the culture and language around him, learning to speak Persian, or Farsi.

Afterward, he traveled with friends before spending two years in Dhahran, Saudi Arabia, teaching at an engineering college. In 1969 he left Saudi Arabia for another long period of travel. Bob, his Peace Corps friend Dick Thompson of Roland, Iowa, and an English companion journeyed through India, Pakistan, the Middle East, and parts of Africa.

One of the most remarkable portions of that journey followed the Nile River. Traveling largely by boat or barge, they followed the river from its headwaters at Lake Victoria northward toward the Mediterranean. Along the way Bob passed through Sudan, visited Luxor and the ruins of ancient Thebes, and eventually reached Alexandria, Egypt, near the mouth of the Nile.

Bob later served a second Peace Corps assignment in a small city in northwestern India. Once again, he immersed himself in the history, culture, and language of the place where he lived. He also spent one or two years teaching English in the Sultanate of Oman.

By the late 1970s, Bob was living and teaching in Thailand. At two different locations, he worked in programs funded by American charitable organizations, teaching English and practical “survival” skills to Vietnamese and other refugees preparing to resettle in the United States.

Travel and language were not Bob’s only pursuits. He remained athletic well into adulthood and was proud that, during the late 1980s, he completed several marathons in Thailand.

In the early 1990s, Bob was diagnosed with Parkinson’s disease. He was determined to continue living as he always had, but the disease gradually limited his physical abilities. During roughly the final eight to ten years of his life, he divided his time between Thailand in the winter and Storm Lake during Iowa’s warmer months.

Bob died in a Bangkok, Thailand, hospital on April 16, 2010, at the age of 70.

He was preceded in death by his parents, Floyd and Edith Kallmer. He was survived by his wife, Fa; Luke Kallmer; his stepmother, Dorcas Kallmer; his brothers Jim and Bill and their families; and other members of the extended Kallmer family.

From a childhood in Storm Lake to classrooms and roads across much of the world, Bob built a life around language, teaching, travel, and an enduring curiosity about people and places far from home.

*Source: Robert C. Kallmer obituary supplied by William F. Kallmer.*
